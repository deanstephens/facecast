# Facecast model catalogue

Every model is generated by a Python script in `scripts/` and exported to
`Assets/_Project/Art/Models/<Name>.fbx` (plus `source/<Name>.blend` and `previews/<Name>.png`).
Unity code looks models up **by name**, and their child objects and anchors **by name**, so the names,
pivots and sizes below are a contract. Change them only together with the Unity side.

Build: `/Applications/Blender.app/Contents/MacOS/Blender -b --factory-startup -P Blender/scripts/build_all.py -- [Name ...]`
(no names = everything). Then run **Facecast ▸ Build ▸ Build Everything** in Unity.

## Conventions (all models)

* 1 Blender unit = 1 m. +Z is up. **The model's front faces -Y**; after export -Y becomes Unity +Z
  (`transform.forward`). A model's own **left** side is Blender **+X**.
* One EMPTY root named exactly like the model, at the origin. Named mesh children (and EMPTY anchors) are
  parented **directly** to it: the FBX exporter's axis conversion misplaces meshes nested under other meshes, so
  any deeper hierarchy is made in Unity (the prefab builder parents `Face` to `Head`). Children have identity
  rotation/scale; their origin is their pivot. Child names must be unique and differ from the model's name.
* Ground-standing models: ground contact at z = 0, footprint centred on the origin.
* Colour comes **only** from the named palette in `scripts/common.py` (`PALETTE`), as vertex colours, and every
  mesh carries the single material `M_Vertex`. Vertex alpha tells Unity's `Facecast/Flat` shader how to treat a
  corner: `Tint`, `TintShade`, `TintDark` are greys multiplied by the avatar's chosen colour; `HoverGlow` and
  `LampGlow` always glow; `Signal` corners glow in the avatar's colour while its player speaks.
* No textures. The one exception is the avatar's `Face`, which has planar 0..1 UVs for the webcam picture. (The
  handwriting on sticky notes and the boards' titles is drawn by Unity, not modelled.)

## Style

Facecast is a sunny town square at golden hour, seen at eye level by people walking around it. Toy-like,
friendly low-poly: smooth rounded avatars, flat-shaded scenery with slight bevels, warm stone, fresh greens.

## Avatar (`models_avatar.py`)

A small hovering figure, 1.93 m to the antenna tip, with a helmet head whose front is a screen.

| Child | Pivot | What Unity does with it |
|---|---|---|
| `Body` | ground, (0, 0, 0) | Hover ring, egg-shaped torso, belt, chest light, collar and neck. Bobs, and leans into movement. |
| `HandL`, `HandR` | hand centres, (±0.41, -0.05, 0.6) | Floating mittens. Swing while walking, drift while idle, lift while talking. |
| `Head` | neck, (0, 0, 1.25) | Helmet, dark bezel, ear pods and antenna (their lights are `Signal`). Pitches with the player's look. |
| `Face` | screen centre, (0, -0.254, 1.5) | **The webcam screen**: a rounded 0.42 × 0.315 m (4:3) panel bulging 1 cm forward, just proud of the bezel. UVs run 0..1 across it, u from the viewer's left to right (+X), v up. Unity parents it to `Head` and draws it with `Facecast/Face`. |
| `Eye` (anchor) | (0, -0.08, 1.5) | Inside the head behind the screen: the first-person eye height. |
| `Nameplate` (anchor) | (0, 0, 2.08) | Where the player's name floats. |

Budget ≤ 5000 tris (now 4880).

## Noticeboards and sticky notes (`models_boards.py`)

Four noticeboards stand round the square; people take sticky notes from the notepads on a board's shelf, write on
them and stick them anywhere on its cork. Unity (`Noticeboard`, `NoteView`) finds these parts by name.

| Model / child | Pivot | What Unity does with it |
|---|---|---|
| `NoticeBoard` | ground, (0, 0, 0) | 2.76 m wide, 2.76 m tall. Faces -Y, like everything. |
| `Frame` | (0, 0, 0) | Posts, rails, backboard, roof, shelf. Its `Tint` corners (roof, title frame) take the board's colour. Mesh collider. |
| `Cork` | the cork's surface centre, (0, -0.03, 1.5) | **2.1 × 1.25 m**, its surface flush with the pivot (the rails stand 2.2 cm proud of it). Notes are stuck on it; Unity reads its size from the mesh bounds and gives it a box collider to point at. |
| `Header` | the plate's front centre, (0, -0.032, 2.305) | Cream title plate, 1.24 × 0.18 m. Unity writes the board's title on it. |
| `PadYellow`, `PadPink`, `PadBlue`, `PadGreen` | the top sheet's centre, (x, -0.145, 0.85) with x = -0.72, -0.24, 0.24, 0.72 | Stacks of notes on the shelf, left to right as you face the board; their colours are Unity's `Notes.Colors` in order. Box colliders: click one to take a note. |
| `StickyNote` | the centre of its back, (0, 0, 0) | One note: 0.2 m square, 1.2 mm thick, its front at y = -0.0012. |
| `Paper` | (0, 0, 0) | The paper, all `Tint` (Unity multiplies by the note's colour). The bottom 3 cm curls up to 7 mm off the board at one corner, so Unity writes only on the flat part above it. |

Board budget ≈ 2100 tris; a note 156.

## The question corner (`models_questions.py`)

A box people post written questions into, with a tray of blank question cards on its side, and a table beside it
where questions are laid once they've been pulled out and read. Unity (`QuestionBox`, `QuestionTable`, `NoteView`)
finds these parts by name.

| Model / child | Pivot | What Unity does with it |
|---|---|---|
| `QuestionBox` | ground, (0, 0, 0) | 1.12 m tall; faces -Y. |
| `Stand` | (0, 0, 0) | Stone base and post. Mesh collider. |
| `Box` | (0, 0, 0) | The box (0.52 × 0.4 × 0.36 m, from 0.72 m up), its lid with the slot (lid and rim `Tint`: the corner's colour), and the tray hanging on its left as you face it. Mesh collider (not a box: the tray of cards lies inside its bounds). Click it to post or pull out a question. |
| `Label` | the plate's face, (0, -0.21, 0.9) | Cream label 0.42 × 0.2 m with a `Tint` border. Unity writes "Questions" and how many are inside. |
| `Cards` | the top of the stack, (-0.36, 0, 1.052) | Blank cards lying in the tray, long side along Y. Box collider: click it to write a question. |
| `QuestionTable` | ground, (0, 0, 0) | 1.5 × 0.85 m, 0.76 m tall; its long side along X, its front facing -Y. |
| `Top` | the tabletop's centre, (0, 0, 0.76) | Box collider. Unity lays cards on it face up, each turned to read the right way up for whoever put it down. |
| `Legs` | (0, 0, 0) | Legs and rails. Mesh collider. |
| `QuestionCard` | the centre of its back, (0, 0, 0) | One index card: 0.2 × 0.14 m, 1.5 mm thick, its front at y = -0.0015. |
| `Card` | (0, 0, 0) | Cream (`CardPaper`, not tinted), with a red rule 4.6 cm above the middle; Unity writes below it. |

Box ≈ 650 tris, table 440, a card 24.
